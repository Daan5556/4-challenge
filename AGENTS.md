# Project guidance

## Context

- This is a school project for the Visioball (called Visiobal in the challenge).
- Daan is working as an IT infrastructure and backend web developer.
- Read [the challenge](docs/sources/challenge.md) for the original assignment.
- The challenge is from Industrial Design Engineering at Fontys Venlo and
  concerns a ball developed for Koninklijke Visio for blind and partially
  sighted users.
- The original assignment focuses on an accessible app, communication with the
  ball, and control of its sounds and settings.

## Project focus

- Focus Daan's contribution on a server platform with social features for the
  Visioball.
- Define how the server platform supports the original challenge and its
  deliverables. Treat the social features as a proposed extension until their
  scope has been agreed.
- Include CDN integration in the infrastructure research and design. Cloudflare
  is a candidate; the provider and services have not been selected.
- Keep accessibility for blind and partially sighted users out ofmind when defining
  platform features and backend behaviour, the app will be used by their guardian.
- Explain infrastructure and backend choices, their trade-offs, and how they
  support the project requirements.

## Documentation

- Write project documentation in Markdown under `docs/`.
- Follow the format of `/home/daan/School/2-challenge/area42/docs`
- Use a descriptive top-level heading, simple second-level headings, and concise
  bullet points for requirements and guidelines.
- Use plain, direct language and consistent terminology. Use nested bullets only
  when they clarify related details.
- Follow the reference's writing format without copying its project-specific
  workflow rules or storage decisions.
- Do not use internal links in project documentation. Refer to internal documents
  and artifacts by their plain-text titles; keep links to external sources.
- Write in first person when describing my role, contribution, choices or
  reflection. Use "I" and "my" instead of referring to Daan by name.
- Do not mention AI assistants or AI assistance in project documentation.
- Distinguish assignment requirements, confirmed decisions, proposals, and open
  questions.
- Keep source documents in `docs/sources/` separate from project conclusions.
- Use several justified research methods based on the DOT framework, as required
  by the Analysis learning outcome. The reference is available at
  `docs/sources/dot-framework.md`.

## Portfolio evidence

- Read [the learning outcomes](docs/sources/learning-outcomes.md) when planning
  work and documenting results. Daan needs portfolio evidence for every outcome
  throughout the project.
- Cover all seven outcomes: Analysis, Advice, Design, Realization, Manage and
  control, Professional Standard, and Personal Leadership.
- Connect planned activities and resulting artifacts to the relevant outcomes.
  Track evidence and coverage gaps in project documentation so missing evidence
  can be addressed during the project.
- For each piece of evidence, record the relevant outcome, Daan's contribution,
  the approach and reasoning, the result, and references to supporting artifacts.
  Include validation, stakeholder feedback, and reflection where relevant.
- Gather evidence as work happens, such as research results, substantiated
  advice, designs and prototype feedback, implementation and test results,
  deployment and monitoring records, stakeholder communication, and personal
  development goals and reflections.
- Treat these examples as possible evidence, not as a replacement for the full
  outcome descriptions. Code alone does not demonstrate every outcome.
- Distinguish planned evidence from completed and validated work. Do not invent
  results, feedback, personal reflections, or claims of outcome achievement.
- Confirm which level applies to Professional Standard and Personal Leadership;
  the source lists both internship and graduation expectations.

## Working guidelines

- Keep the work appropriate for a school project and Daan's infrastructure and
  backend role.
- Ask targeted questions when missing requirements affect scope or architecture.
- Do not present unconfirmed features, technologies, or research outcomes as
  established decisions.
- Document relevant validation and its outcome when implementing functionality
  or infrastructure.
